# E004: state tracking. Commuting vs noncommuting holonomy, and why the relations matter. Registration

Status: **Registered** (2026-09-27), before any E004 code was written. Results go in `RESULTS.md`.

## Why this experiment

E002 asked for information that "no equal-cost classical recurrence" can compute (E7). Taken
literally, that cannot exist here: a holonomy *is* a classical recurrence (a matrix product), and a
lookup table does any finite-group task in O(1) per step. So E004 asks a sharper question that
bears on current machine learning.

**Which kinds of recurrent state can track a noncommutative state, and what must hold for it?**

A number of recent sequence models (diagonal state-space models and linear RNNs) update their state
with **commuting** transition matrices. Merrill, Petty & Sabharwal (ICML 2024, "The Illusion of
State in State-Space Models") showed such models cannot express state tracking over non-solvable
groups such as S5. E002's ABELIAN null is exactly that class. E004 puts the known result on this
instrument, with controls, and adds a third arm that E002 predicts nothing about: operators that do
**not** commute but do **not** satisfy the task's relations either.

What E004 claims as new is only the instrument and the controls. The expressivity results are
prior work.

## Task (fixed before running)

- The group S5 (permutations of 5 items, 120 elements), generated by t = the transposition (0 1)
  and c = the 5-cycle (0 1 2 3 4).
- A **word** is L uniformly random letters from {t, c}. Its **label** is the permutation the word
  composes to (applied left to right): 120 classes.
- L ∈ {10, 40, 160}. For each L and each seed 1-3: 6000 training words and 2000 test words.
- **Readout (the same for every operator arm):** nearest class centroid. Average each class's
  holonomy over the training words; classify a test word by the nearest centroid (Frobenius norm).

## Arms

- **REP.** The 5 × 5 permutation matrices of t and c. This is a faithful representation: the
  operators satisfy every relation of S5.
- **DIAG.** Random diagonal unitaries, dimension 32 (commuting; more dimensions than REP). A
  diagonal holonomy depends only on how many t's and c's a word contains.
- **COUNT-CEILING.** For each letter count seen in training, predict the most frequent training
  label with that count; for a count never seen, predict the overall most frequent label. This is
  the ceiling for **any** commuting-operator arm with any readout, estimated on held-out words.
- **RANDOM-U.** Random 5 × 5 unitaries exp(iH) for t and c. They do not commute, and they satisfy
  none of S5's relations.
- **SHAM.** P8's sham applied to REP: keep each generator's eigenvalues, randomise its eigenvectors.
  The sham keeps t² = 1 and c⁵ = 1, because those follow from the eigenvalues. It breaks the relations
  that tie t and c together.
- **TABLE.** A 120-state lookup table, updated once per letter: the cheap exact classical baseline.

## Registered predictions

- **G1** REP test accuracy = 1.0000 at every L and seed, including L = 160, 16× the shortest.
- **G2** DIAG: holonomies of words with equal letter counts agree to < 1e-12. Accuracy is
  ≤ COUNT-CEILING + 0.02 at L = 40 and 160.
- **G3** COUNT-CEILING < 0.05 at L = 40 and 160. Letter counts give away the sign, and little else.
- **G4** RANDOM-U accuracy < 0.05 at L = 40 and 160. Noncommutativity alone is not enough.
- **G5** SHAM: t² = 1 and c⁵ = 1 hold to < 1e-12. The relation (tc)^k = 1 (k = the order of tc in S5,
  computed by the run) is violated by > 1e-3. Accuracy < 0.05 at L = 40 and 160. The E002 sham that
  failed as a control is the right control here, because this task depends on the relations and
  not only on noncommutativity.
- **G6** TABLE = 1.0000 everywhere. **No advantage over classical computation is claimed.** The
  registered finding concerns the commuting-state class (diagonal recurrences), not classical
  computers.

## Unrun, the questions E004 hands on

- **E004-U1.** Learn the operators from (word, label) data alone, with gradient descent on unitary
  parameters, starting from random. Does training find a representation that satisfies the
  relations, and does it generalise by length where a learned diagonal model cannot? That is the
  part that would touch real models.
- **E004-U2.** A task whose relations are not known in advance: can the relation-violation measure
  in G5 detect, from data, whether a learned model has found the task's group?

## Prior art

Barrington (1989), width-5 branching programs over S5; Merrill, Petty & Sabharwal (2024), "The
Illusion of State in State-Space Models"; Grazzi et al. (2024) on negative eigenvalues in linear
RNNs. E004 is a controlled reproduction of that line on this instrument, not a new result about it.
